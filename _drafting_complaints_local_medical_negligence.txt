## Facts of the Case

1. The Plaintiff, Mrs. Anjali Deshmukh, is a law-abiding citizen of India, aged 42 years, and a resident of Pune. The Plaintiff maintains a good reputation and standing in the community.

2. The Defendant No. 1, Dr. Rakesh Nair, is a medical practitioner and surgeon who, at all material times, was employed by or practicing at the Defendant No. 2 hospital. The Defendant No. 2, XYZ Multispecialty Hospital, is a corporate entity providing medical and healthcare services to the public for consideration and holds itself out as possessing specialized skills and infrastructure.

3. That in or around early March 2024, the Plaintiff was suffering from a gallbladder-related ailment. Upon medical advice, she approached the Defendant No. 2 hospital for consultation and treatment. The Plaintiff was placed under the care and supervision of Defendant No. 1, who, after examination, advised her to undergo a gallbladder removal surgery (cholecystectomy).

4. Relying upon the representations and assurances of skill and care made by the Defendants, the Plaintiff got admitted to the Defendant No. 2 hospital. On 12th March 2024, the Defendant No. 1 performed the gallbladder surgery on the Plaintiff.

5. It is submitted that immediately following the surgery, the Plaintiff, instead of recovering, began to experience severe and debilitating abdominal pain, discomfort, and other adverse symptoms, which were not typical post-operative manifestations for such a procedure. The Plaintiff and her family members repeatedly brought these complications to the notice of Defendant No. 1 and the staff at Defendant No. 2 hospital.

6. However, the Defendants failed to properly diagnose the cause of the Plaintiff's deteriorating condition and dismissed her complaints as routine post-operative pain. Due to the unceasing pain and worsening health, the Plaintiff was compelled to seek a second opinion and was subsequently admitted to another hospital in Pune.

7. Upon investigation at the second hospital, it was discovered that the Plaintiff's bile duct had been severely damaged during the surgery performed by Defendant No. 1. This iatrogenic injury was the direct cause of her post-operative complications. To rectify this grave error, the Plaintiff had to undergo a complex and high-risk corrective surgery.

8. As a direct consequence of the negligence of the Defendants, the Plaintiff was forced to incur additional medical expenses amounting to approximately ₹3,50,000/- (Rupees Three Lakh Fifty Thousand only) for the diagnostic tests, corrective surgery, and subsequent hospitalization and treatment.

9. It is submitted that the negligent act of Defendant No. 1 and the subsequent failure of the Defendants to manage the complications have caused the Plaintiff immense and unbearable physical pain, prolonged suffering, and severe mental and emotional trauma. The Plaintiff has been unable to resume her normal life and professional duties, leading to further financial and personal losses.

10. The Plaintiff, through her family members, made several attempts to follow up with the Defendants, seeking an
explanation for the botched surgery and compensation for the harm caused. However, the Defendants have consistently
refused to acknowledge their negligence, have not provided the Plaintiff's complete medical records despite requests,
and have offered no compensation, thereby displaying a complete lack of professional accountability and empathy. This
has left the Plaintiff with no other remedy but to approach this Hon'ble Court.

## Legal Reasoning and Case Law

11. It is submitted that a doctor-patient relationship existed between the Plaintiff and Defendant No. 1, and a
hospital-patient relationship existed with Defendant No. 2. This relationship imposes a non-negotiable duty of care upon
the Defendants to treat the Plaintiff with a reasonable degree of skill, care, and diligence. The Supreme Court in
*Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha*, (1995) 6 SCC 651, has unequivocally held that medical services for
consideration fall within the ambit of 'service' as defined under the Consumer Protection Act, and that medical
practitioners have a duty of care towards their patients. The principles of duty of care are equally, if not more,
stringent in a suit for damages under the law of torts.

12. The standard of care required from a medical professional is that of a reasonably competent practitioner in that
field. Defendant No. 1, being a qualified surgeon, was expected to possess and exercise the skill and knowledge of a
prudent surgeon performing a cholecystectomy. The duty of care extends to pre-operative, operative, and post-operative
stages. The Defendant No. 2 hospital, by providing the infrastructure and staff, owed an independent and non-delegable
duty of care to the Plaintiff to ensure her safety and well-being.

13. The act of causing damage to the Plaintiff's bile duct during a standard gallbladder surgery constitutes a patent and egregious breach of the said duty of care. A bile duct injury is a known but largely avoidable complication, and its occurrence in this case is indicative of a lack of requisite skill, attention, and care on the part of Defendant No. 1 during the surgical procedure. This is not a mere error of judgment but an act of gross negligence.

14. The facts of this case attract the doctrine of *res ipsa loquitur*, which means "the thing speaks for itself". The
injury to the Plaintiff is of a kind that does not ordinarily occur in the absence of negligence. The surgical
instruments and the entire procedure were under the exclusive control of Defendant No. 1 and his team at Defendant No.
2's facility. Therefore, the burden of proof shifts to the Defendants to demonstrate that the injury occurred despite
them having taken all reasonable care.

15. The negligence of the Defendants is the direct and proximate cause of the injuries and losses suffered by the Plaintiff. The chain of causation is unbroken: the negligent surgery led to the bile duct injury, which in turn led to severe pain, the necessity of a second corrective surgery, additional financial expenditure, and immense physical and mental agony. But for the Defendants' breach of duty, the Plaintiff would not have suffered these damages.

16. The Defendant No. 2 hospital is vicariously liable for the negligent acts of its employee/agent, Defendant No. 1.
The surgery was performed on the hospital's premises, using its facilities, and the Plaintiff had approached the
hospital for treatment, which was then assigned to Defendant No. 1. In *Savita Garg v. Director, National Heart
Institute*, (2004) 8 SCC 56, the Supreme Court held that a hospital is vicariously liable for the acts of its doctors,
whether they are employees or consultants.

17. The Supreme Court in *Dr. Laxman Balkrishna Joshi v. Dr. Trimbak Bapu Godbole*, AIR 1969 SC 128, laid down the duties of a doctor towards a patient, which include (a) a duty of care in deciding whether to undertake the case, (b) a duty of care in deciding what treatment to give, and (c) a duty of care in the administration of that treatment. The Defendants have failed on all counts, particularly in the administration of the surgical treatment and in the post-operative care.

18. Furthermore, the Supreme Court in *Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab*, (2005) 6 SCC 1, while distinguishing between
civil and criminal negligence, affirmed that for civil liability (a suit for damages), a professional can be held liable
if they did not possess the requisite skill which they professed to have, or if they failed to exercise, with reasonable
competence in the given case, the skill which they did possess. The actions of Defendant No. 1 fall squarely within this
definition of actionable negligence.

19. The conduct of the Defendants is also in violation of the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, which obligates medical practitioners to provide competent service, uphold the dignity of the profession, and be accountable for their actions. The refusal to acknowledge the error and provide records is a further breach of these ethical duties.

20. In light of the aforesaid facts, established legal principles, and binding precedents, it is submitted that the Defendants were grossly negligent in their treatment of the Plaintiff. Their acts and omissions directly resulted in significant physical, emotional, and financial harm to the Plaintiff, for which they are jointly and severally liable to pay damages.
