## Legal Reasoning and Case Law

15. It is submitted that the relationship between the Plaintiff and the Defendants was that of a patient and her medical

16. The standard of care in medical negligence cases is well-established. It is the standard of the ordinary skilled
person exercising and professing to have that special skill. A medical professional is not expected to be perfect, but
they must adhere to the practices accepted as proper by a responsible body of medical professionals skilled in that
particular art. This principle, originating from the *Bolam test*, has been adopted and refined by Indian courts. A
deviation from this standard constitutes a breach of the duty of care.

17. It is submitted that Defendant No. 1 committed a flagrant breach of his duty of care. Causing damage to the bile
duct during a routine cholecystectomy is a known complication, but its occurrence is often indicative of a failure to

injury suffered by the Plaintiff was not a result of an unforeseen complication but a direct consequence of the
surgeon's substandard performance. The facts of this case attract the doctrine of *res ipsa loquitur*—the thing speaks
for itself—as such an injury would not have occurred in the ordinary course of events if the operating surgeon had
exercised proper care.

18. The Hon'ble Supreme Court of India, in the landmark case of *Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab, (2005) 6 SCC 1*, while
delineating the difference between civil and criminal negligence, reiterated the standard for civil liability. The Court
held that a professional can be held liable for negligence if they (a) did not possess the requisite skill which they
professed to have, or (b) failed to exercise, with reasonable competence in the given case, the skill which they did
possess. The actions of Defendant No. 1 fall squarely within this definition of actionable negligence.

19. It is further submitted that the principle of causation is clearly established. The breach of duty by Defendant No. 1 was the direct and proximate cause of the injuries and losses suffered by the Plaintiff. 20. The Defendant No. 2 hospital is vicariously liable for the negligent acts of Defendant No. 1. A hospital that offers
medical services to the public has a non-delegable duty to provide care of a certain standard. It is liable for the
negligence of its doctors, whether they are full-time employees or visiting consultants, as the patient seeks services
from the institution itself. The hospital cannot absolve itself of responsibility for the negligence of the doctors
practicing within its premises.

21. The Hon'ble Supreme Court in *Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha, (1995) 6 SCC 651*, held that medical services fall under the definition of 'service' under the Consumer Protection Act, and that hospitals are liable for the negligence of their doctors. The Court observed that the contract is one for services and that the hospital is responsible for the actions of the doctors it provides, thereby cementing the principle of vicarious liability in the context of medical services.
