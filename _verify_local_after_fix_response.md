### Applicable Offences under Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)

Based on the actions described, the following offences under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) are applicable:

2.  **Cheating:** If the hacker, by deceiving the company or its users, induced them to deliver any property or to do or omit to do anything which caused damage or harm, it could amount to cheating. 3.  **Criminal Breach of Trust:** If the hacker was entrusted with any dominion over the company's servers or data (e.g., as an employee or contractor), and he dishonestly misappropriated or converted that property for his own use, or dishonestly used or disposed of that property in violation of any law or contract, it would constitute criminal breach of trust.

### Provisions of the Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act)

The Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act) is the primary legislation in India dealing with cybercrime and electronic commerce. The hacker's actions would attract several provisions of this Act:

1.  **Penalty for damage to computer, computer system, etc.:** The law imposes civil liability for unauthorized access, downloading, extracting, or copying data from a computer resource. The hacker's actions directly fall under accessing without permission, downloading/copying data (Aadhaar, PAN, bank, credit card, source code), and stealing source code. The penalty can extend to significant compensation to the affected person.
2.  **Tampering with computer source documents:** The theft of source code directly falls under this offence, which penalizes anyone who knowingly or intentionally conceals, destroys, or alters any computer source code used for a computer, computer program, computer system, or computer network.
3.  **Computer related offences:** If any person, dishonestly or fraudulently, commits acts of unauthorized access and data theft, he shall be punishable with imprisonment, or with a fine, or with both. ### Digital Evidence Collection

Digital evidence must be collected meticulously to maintain its integrity and admissibility:

1.  **Identification:** Identify all potential sources of digital evidence (servers, workstations, mobile devices, cloud storage, network devices).
2.  **Preservation:** Prevent any alteration or destruction of data. This involves isolating devices, disconnecting them from networks, and creating forensic images.
3.  **Collection:** Use specialized forensic tools to acquire data. This includes:
    -   **Disk Imaging:** Creating bit-for-bit copies of hard drives and other storage media.
    -   **Memory Forensics:** Capturing the volatile memory (RAM) of live systems.
    -   **Network Packet Capture:** Recording network traffic to analyze communication patterns.
    -   **Log Collection:** Gathering logs from various systems.
4.  **Documentation:** Document every step of the collection process, including who collected what, when, where, and how, along with hash values of the collected data.
5.  **Chain of Custody:** Maintain a strict chain of custody to track the movement and handling of digital evidence from the moment it's collected until it's presented in court.

### Preservation of Electronic Evidence

Preservation is critical to ensure the integrity and authenticity of electronic evidence:

1.  **Forensic Duplication:** Create exact, bit-for-bit copies (forensic images) of original storage media. This ensures the original evidence remains untouched.
2.  **Hashing:** Generate cryptographic hash values (e.g., MD5, SHA-256) of the original data and the forensic image. Any change to the data will result in a different hash value, proving tampering.
3.  **Write-Blockers:** Use hardware or software write-blockers during acquisition to prevent any accidental modification of the original evidence.
4.  **Secure Storage:** Store electronic evidence in a secure environment with restricted access to prevent unauthorized access or alteration.
5.  **Documentation:** Maintain detailed records of all preservation activities, including dates, times, personnel involved, and tools used.

### Role of Cyber Forensic Laboratory

Cyber forensic laboratories play a crucial role in analyzing digital evidence:

-   **Expert Analysis:** Forensic experts in these labs analyze the collected digital evidence to reconstruct events, identify attack vectors, extract deleted data, and attribute actions to specific individuals.
-   **Tool Expertise:** They use specialized forensic software and hardware to analyze various types of digital evidence, including operating systems, file systems, network protocols, and application data.
-   **Report Generation:** They prepare detailed forensic reports that explain their findings, methodologies, and conclusions, which are crucial for legal proceedings.
-   **Expert Testimony:** Forensic experts provide expert testimony in court to explain complex technical details to judges and juries.
-   **Research and Development:** They also engage in research and development to stay updated with new cybercrime techniques and forensic methodologies.

### Relevant Supreme Court Judgments

While specific judgments under the new BNS and BSA are yet to emerge, the Supreme Court has laid down important principles regarding electronic evidence under the Indian Evidence Act and IT Act.

-   ***State (NCT of Delhi) v. Navjot Sandhu alias Afsan Guru, (2005) 11 SCC 600:*** This case, related to the Parliament attack, discussed the admissibility of call detail records and other electronic evidence. The Court emphasized the need for proper authentication of electronic records.
-   ***Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer and Others, (2014) 10 SCC 473:*** This landmark judgment clarified the procedure for admitting electronic evidence under Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, making the certificate mandatory for secondary electronic evidence.
-   ***Shafhi Mohammad v. The State of Himachal Pradesh, (2018) 2 SCC 801:*** This case somewhat relaxed the strict requirement of the Section 65B certificate in certain circumstances, particularly when the electronic device is not in the possession of the party seeking to produce the evidence. However, *Anvar P.V.* remains the primary authority.
-   ***P. Gopalkrishnan alias Dileep v. The State of Kerala, (2019) 11 SCC 560:*** This case reiterated the importance of Section 65B certificate for the admissibility of electronic evidence.

These judgments highlight the Supreme Court's emphasis on ensuring the authenticity, integrity, and reliability of electronic evidence for its admissibility in court.
