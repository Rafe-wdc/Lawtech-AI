### ASHARAFI DEVI (D) THR. LRS. VS STATE OF U.P. THROUGH COLLECTOR/DISTRICT MAGISTRATE, 01-02-2019

### Key Legal Issues
- Whether a review application can be entertained for every factual or legal error.
- The scope of "error apparent on the face of the record" under Order 47 Rule 1 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.
- The power of the Supreme Court to invoke Article 142 of the Constitution to permit a challenge to a main order when only a review order was appealed.

### Detailed Narrative
The original appellant was the owner of lands subjected to ceiling proceedings under the Urban Land (Ceiling and
Regulation) Act, 1976, which resulted in some lands being declared surplus. The State claimed possession of the surplus
land in 1982. After the Ceiling Act was repealed for Uttar Pradesh in 1999, the appellant filed a writ petition in 2002,
claiming that the ceiling proceedings lapsed as she remained in possession. The High Court dismissed the writ petition
on March 14, 2008, finding that the appellant failed to prove possession. The appellant then filed a review application,
which the High Court dismissed on December 16, 2008. The present appeal was filed against this review order.

### Court Observations
The Supreme Court observed that the appellant had only challenged the review order and not the main order dismissing the writ petition. The Court declined to invoke its extraordinary powers under Article 142 of the Constitution to allow a challenge to the main order, citing the appellant's delay and lack of legal impediment in filing a separate Special Leave Petition against the main order.

Regarding the review application, the Court reiterated that every factual or legal error cannot be the subject matter of
review under Order 47 Rule 1 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. For a review to be permissible, the error or mistake
must be "apparent on the face of the record." The High Court, in its review order, had correctly held that the main
order did not contain any such apparent factual or legal error. The Supreme Court affirmed that a finding of fact, such
as the failure to prove possession, could not be re-examined de novo in review jurisdiction like an appellate court.

### Judgment & Direction
The Supreme Court concurred with the High Court's reasoning and conclusion, dismissing the appeal. The Court held that the review order was passed in conformity with the requirements of Order 47 Rule 1 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and that the main order did not contain any factual or legal error apparent on the face of the record to warrant a review.

### COIMBATORE DISTRICT PODU THOZILLAR SAMGAMREPRESENTED BY ITS VS BALA SUBRAMANIA FOUNDRY AND ORS., 11-08-1987

### Key Legal Issues
- The grounds for interference with an arbitrator's award under the Arbitration Act, 1940.
- Distinction between errors of fact and errors of law in an arbitration award.
- What constitutes an "error apparent on the face of the record" in an arbitration award.

### Detailed Narrative
Disputes arose between the partners of Bala Subramania Foundry, leading to legal proceedings that were eventually
referred to an Arbitrator by the Supreme Court. The Arbitrator filed an award. The petitioner in the special leave
petitions alleged legal misconduct by the Arbitrator and claimed there were errors amenable to correction by the Supreme
Court, arguing the award was inconsistent and should be set aside. Separately, workers contended their claims were not
fully protected, citing an erroneous estimation of gratuity.

### Court Observations
The Supreme Court clarified the settled law regarding interference with arbitration awards. Relying on precedents like
*Union of India v. A.L. Rallia Ram, [1964] 3 S.C.R. 164* and *Champsey Bhara and Company v. Jivraj Balloo Spinning and
Weaving Company Ltd., [1932] L.R. 50 I.A. 324*, the Court reiterated that civil courts cannot exercise appellate powers
over an arbitrator's decision. An award can only be set aside if there is an "error of law on the face of it," meaning a
legal proposition that is the basis of the award and is erroneous, found within the award itself or a document
incorporated into it. Mistakes of fact, even if true, do not amount to an error apparent on the face of the record. The
Court found no erroneous legal proposition in the award or annexed documents.

### Judgment & Direction
The Supreme Court dismissed the objections to the award, finding no error of law apparent on the face of the record. The Court held that the alleged mistakes were, at best, errors of fact, which are not justiciable in an application before the court. The award was made a rule of the Court, and judgment was passed in terms of the award, with interest on the judgment at 9%.
