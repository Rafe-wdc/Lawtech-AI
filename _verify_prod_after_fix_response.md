### Admissibility of Electronic Evidence under Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA)

The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, and introduced significant provisions for the admissibility of electronic evidence.

1.  **Section 61 BSA:** States that an electronic or digital record cannot be denied admissibility simply because it is electronic, and such a record has the same legal effect and enforceability as any other document, subject to Section 63 BSA.
    -   **Old Provision:** Section 3 of The Indian Evidence Act, 1872
    -   **New Provision:** Section 2 of The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023
2.  **Section 63 BSA (replacing Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act):** This is the key provision governing the
admissibility of electronic records. It states that any information contained in an electronic record, which is printed
on paper, stored, recorded, or copied in optical or magnetic media or semiconductor memory, and produced by a computer
or any communication device, shall be deemed to be a document. For an electronic record to be admissible without
producing the original device, it must be accompanied by a certificate.
    -   **Old Provision:** Section 65B of The Indian Evidence Act, 1872
-   **New Provision:** Section 63 of The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023: "(1)Notwithstanding anything contained in
this Adhiniyam, any information contained in an electronic record, which is printed on a paper, stored, recorded or
copied in optical or magnetic media or semiconductor memory, produced by a computer or any communication device, in such
form as may be prescribed, shall be deemed to be also a document, if the conditions mentioned in this section are
satisfied in relation to the information and computer or the communication device in question."
    -   The certificate must be signed by both the person in charge of the device and an expert.
    -   It must disclose the record's hash value, which is the digital fingerprint of the file.
    -   The conditions for admissibility include that the computer system was operating properly, the information was regularly fed into it, and the information was derived in the ordinary course of activities.
3.  **Opinion of Examiner of Electronic Evidence (Section 39(2) BSA):** This section allows for the opinion of an examiner of electronic evidence to be considered relevant.
    -   **Old Provision:** Section 45A of The Indian Evidence Act, 1872
    -   **New Provision:** Section 39(2) of The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023: "When in a proceeding, the court has to form an opinion on any matter relating to any information transmitted or stored in any computer resource or any other electronic or digital form, the opinion of the Examiner of Electronic Evidence referred to in section 79A of the Information Technology Act, 2000, is a relevant fact."
