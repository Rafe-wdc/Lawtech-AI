### Detailed Narrative

The case originated from a well-merited judgment passed by the High Court of Judicature at Hyderabad, which confirmed a
trial court's decision. This judgment was subsequently reviewed by the High Court, acting like an appellate court, based

-   **INDIAN AND EASTERN NEWSPAPER SOCIETY NEW DELHI VS COMMISSIONER OF INCOME TAX, NEW DELHI**
The assessee, Indian and Eastern Newspaper Society, a professional association, owned a building with a conference hall
and rooms let out on rent. The income from this source was consistently assessed as business income for several
assessment years. Subsequently, the internal audit organization of the Income Tax Department opined that this income

The appellants, lessors of a cinema theatre, leased the property to the first respondent for three years. Upon the

-   **INDIAN AND EASTERN NEWSPAPER SOCIETY NEW DELHI VS COMMISSIONER OF INCOME TAX, NEW DELHI**
The Supreme Court meticulously analyzed the term "information" in Section 147(b) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. It
clarified that "information" must be derived from an external source and pertain to facts or the true and correct state

party's function is administrative, not judicial, and its opinion on a point of law does not constitute "information"
for reopening assessments. The Court stressed that the Income Tax Officer must independently form a belief that income
has escaped assessment based on legally recognized information.
