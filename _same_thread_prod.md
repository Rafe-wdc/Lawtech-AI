## VI. Evidence

The Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (replaced by the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023), governs the admissibility, relevance, and proof of facts.

### A. Types of Evidence
*   **Oral Evidence**: Statements by witnesses in court, which must be direct.
*   **Documentary Evidence**: Documents produced for court inspection.
*   **Circumstantial Evidence**: Relies on inference to connect to a conclusion of fact. For conviction, circumstances must form a complete chain, ruling out any other hypothesis except guilt.

### B. Burden of Proof
In criminal cases, the prosecution bears the burden of proving guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The accused is presumed innocent until proven guilty.

    *   **Lawyer's Role**: A lawyer ensures these rights are upheld, communicates with the arrested person, and takes steps to secure their release on bail.

### III. During Investigation

A lawyer's presence during the investigation can prevent procedural irregularities and protect the accused from self-incrimination.

*   **Statements to Police**: Section 162 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 181 of the BNSS, 2023) states that statements made to a police officer during investigation, if reduced to writing, shall not be signed by the person making it and generally cannot be used as evidence, except for contradiction. ### IV. Post-Investigation and Trial

Once the investigation is complete and a charge sheet is filed, a lawyer is essential for navigating the trial process.

*   **Charge Sheet and Cognizance**: Section 173 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 193 of the BNSS, 2023) mandates the police to submit a report (charge sheet) upon completion of investigation. The Magistrate then takes cognizance under Section 190 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 223 of the BNSS, 2023).
    *   **Lawyer's Role**: A lawyer reviews the charge sheet, identifies weaknesses in the prosecution's case, and prepares the defense strategy. They represent the accused throughout the trial, including framing of charges, cross-examination of prosecution witnesses, presenting defense evidence, and making final arguments.

## I. Pre-Arrest Stage

The pre-arrest stage is a critical juncture where legal advice is indispensable to safeguard an individual's rights and interests.

### A. Upon Apprehension of a Complaint or Investigation

Even before an official First Information Report (FIR) is registered, if an individual apprehends that a criminal complaint might be filed against them, or that they are under police scrutiny, consulting a lawyer is crucial. A lawyer can assess the potential allegations, advise on the legal implications, and strategise proactive measures. This may include gathering evidence, preparing for potential questioning, or initiating steps to seek protective legal remedies.

### B. During the Registration of a First Information Report (FIR)

1.  **When an FIR is Filed Against an Individual**: Upon learning that an FIR has been registered under Section 154 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 173 of the BNSS, 2023) for a cognizable offence, immediate legal consultation is essential. A lawyer can:
    *   Ascertain the specific allegations and sections of law invoked.
    *   Advise on the implications of the FIR and the likely course of police investigation.
    *   Guide the individual on their rights during the investigation, including the right against self-incrimination and the right to legal representation during questioning.
    *   Prepare and file an application for anticipatory bail, if appropriate.

2.  **When Seeking to Register an FIR**: If an individual is the victim of a cognizable offence and the police refuse to register an FIR under Section 154 of the CrPC, a lawyer can assist in pursuing the available legal remedies. This includes:
    *   Sending a written complaint to the Superintendent of Police or Deputy Commissioner of Police under Section 154(3) of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 173(4) of the BNSS, 2023).
    *   Filing a complaint before a Magistrate under Section 156(3) read with Section 190 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 223 of the BNSS, 2023), seeking directions for the police to register an FIR and investigate.
    *   For non-cognizable offences, a lawyer can guide the informant in approaching the Magistrate as required by Section 155 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 174 of the BNSS, 2023), since the police cannot investigate without a Magistrate's order.

### C. Seeking Anticipatory Bail

The provision for anticipatory bail under Section 438 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 484 of the BNSS, 2023) allows a person apprehending arrest for a non-bailable offence to seek pre-arrest bail from the High Court or the Court of Session. This is a critical stage where legal expertise is indispensable. A lawyer can:
*   Draft and file the anticipatory bail application, articulating the grounds for relief.
*   Present arguments before the court regarding the nature and gravity of the accusation, the applicant's antecedents, and the absence of flight risk or tampering with evidence/witnesses.
*   Address the factors considered by courts, as highlighted by the Supreme Court in *Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. The Supreme Court has clarified that anticipatory bail should not invariably be limited to a fixed period and can continue until the end of the trial, unless there are specific reasons to limit its tenure, as reiterated in *Sushila Aggarwal & Ors. v. ## II. During Investigation and Arrest

While the primary focus is pre-arrest, a lawyer's presence remains vital during the investigation and if an arrest occurs.

*   **Police Questioning**: A lawyer can advise on responses during police interrogation, ensuring that no self-incriminating statements are made and that the individual's rights are protected. Section 162 of the CrPC (corresponding to Section 181 of the BNSS, 2023) states that statements made to a police officer during investigation, if reduced to writing, shall not be signed by the person making it and generally cannot be used as evidence, except for contradiction. The BNSS, 2023, refers to Section 148 of the BSA, 2023, for contradiction.
*   **Upon Arrest**: If an arrest is made, immediate legal assistance is necessary to ensure that the arrested person's constitutional and statutory rights are upheld, including the right to be informed of the grounds of arrest (Section 50(1) CrPC, corresponding to Section 44(1) BNSS, 2023), the right to consult a legal practitioner, and the right to be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours (Section 57 CrPC, corresponding to Section 48(1) BNSS, 2023), as mandated by Article 22(1) and 22(2) of the Constitution of India. The guidelines laid down in *D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal, (1997) 1 SCC 416*, regarding police conduct during arrest and detention, can be enforced through legal counsel. The Supreme Court in *TUSHARBHAI RAJNIKANTBHAI SHAH v. STATE OF GUJARAT, SLP(Crl) No.-014489 - 2023* ([Judgment PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2023/44034/44034_2023_3_301_54676_Judgement_07-Aug-2024.pdf)) firmly stated that once anticipatory bail is granted, the Investigating Officer does not have a blanket liberty to seek police custody remand.

## III. Territorial Jurisdiction for Anticipatory Bail

The Supreme Court in *PRIYA INDORIA v. THE STATE OF KARNATAKA, Crl.A. No.-003549-003552 - 2023* ([Judgment PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2023/7943/7943_2023_14_1501_48299_Judgement_20-Nov-2023.pdf)) addressed the issue of "extra-territorial anticipatory bail" or "transit anticipatory bail." It held that while the expression "the High Court or the Court of Session" in Section 438 CrPC does not restrict jurisdiction to only where the FIR is lodged, it does not mean an accused can seek full-fledged anticipatory bail in any state. However, to ensure access to justice and protect personal liberty, a court can grant "transit anticipatory bail" or "limited interim protection" for a fixed period, allowing the accused to approach the competent court in the state where the FIR is registered for regular anticipatory bail. This power should be exercised in exceptional and compelling circumstances, with reasons recorded.
