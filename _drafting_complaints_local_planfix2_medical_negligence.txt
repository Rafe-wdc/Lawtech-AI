## Legal Reasoning and Case Law

13. It is submitted that the relationship between the Plaintiff (patient) and the Defendants (doctor and hospital) is one of trust, wherein the Defendants owed the Plaintiff a duty of care. This duty required them to possess and exercise the degree of skill, knowledge, and care that a reasonably competent medical professional would exercise under similar circumstances. This fundamental principle is the bedrock of the law of medical negligence.

14. The act of causing severe injury to the Plaintiff's bile duct during a standard gallbladder surgery constitutes a
patent breach of the aforesaid duty of care. This is not a case of an error of judgment but one of gross negligence,
falling far below the accepted standard of surgical practice. The doctrine of *res ipsa loquitur* (the thing speaks for
itself) is squarely applicable to the facts of this case, as such an injury does not occur in the ordinary course of
events if the surgeon has exercised proper care. The onus, therefore, shifts to the Defendants to prove that they were
not negligent.

15. The Hon’ble Supreme Court of India, in the landmark case of *Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab & Anr.*, (2005) 6 SCC
1, while discussing the standard of care, held that a professional may be held liable for negligence if they did not
possess the requisite skill which they professed to have, or if they did not exercise, with reasonable competence in the
given case, the skill which they did possess. In the present case, Defendant No. 1's actions demonstrate a clear failure
to exercise the skill expected of a qualified surgeon.

16. It is further submitted that Defendant No. 2, XYZ Multispecialty Hospital, is vicariously liable for the negligent
acts of Defendant No. 1. Defendant No. 1 was operating within the hospital's premises, using its facilities, and was
either an employee or a consultant engaged by the hospital. The hospital holds itself out to the public as a provider of
medical services and is therefore responsible for ensuring that the care provided meets a certain standard. The Hon'ble
Supreme Court in *Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha*, (1995) 6 SCC 651, firmly established that hospitals are
liable for the negligence of their doctors, whether they are employees or consultants.

17. The negligence of the Defendants is the direct and proximate cause of the injuries and losses suffered by the Plaintiff. The chain of causation is unbroken: the negligent surgery led to the bile duct injury, which in turn led to severe pain, the necessity of a second major surgery, substantial financial expenditure, and immense physical and mental suffering. But for the Defendants' breach of duty, the Plaintiff would not have suffered these damages.

18. The duties of a medical practitioner towards a patient have been well-articulated by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in
*Dr. Laxman Balkrishna Joshi v. Dr. Trimbak Bapu Godbole*, AIR 1969 SC 128. The Court held that a doctor has a duty to
decide whether to undertake the case, what treatment to give, and to administer that treatment with a reasonable degree
of skill and care. The Defendants in the present case have failed on all counts post the initial decision to undertake
the surgery, particularly in the administration of the surgical procedure and the post-operative management.

from the said injury. The total claim of Rs. 25,00,000/- is fair, just, and reasonable in the circumstances of the case.
